\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This manual develops, end to end, the modelling choices for predicting private-company
success and financing activity from commercial deal data (PitchBook) and standardized
macroeconomic series (FactSet). It is organised around a single practical axis: \emph{how
much does the observation process let us see?} In the European regime, comprehensive
statutory reporting makes firm fundamentals (EBITDA, revenue) directly observable, and the
problem is supervised prediction with careful data engineering, gradient boosting, and a
causal layer built on the backdoor criterion with bootstrap uncertainty quantification
(Part~I). In the American regime, fundamentals are largely unobserved; deal marks
(valuations, rounds, employees) and macro factors must proxy for growth, which changes both
the estimand and the identification strategy (Part~II). Finally, because the data update
\emph{only at deal events}, the natural formulation is the calibration of counting
processes: inhomogeneous Poisson models with covariates, self-exciting and self-inhibiting
extensions, Hawkes processes and their two-stage marked decomposition, and
interval-censored (mean-behaviour) approximations when timestamps are unreliable
(Part~III). Throughout, we give precise mathematical statements, identifiability analyses,
uncertainty quantification recipes, and decision tables for choosing between methods; all
quantitative claims are validated on a fully specified synthetic testbed that ships with
the accompanying code.
\end{abstract}

\chapter*{How to read this manual}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{How to read this manual}

The three parts are ordered by decreasing observability of the firm's state, and each can
be read on its own. A reader who only works with European portfolios needs
Chapters~\ref{ch:data}--\ref{ch:causal}; a reader focused on US venture data can skim
Part~I for the data-engineering and uncertainty-quantification machinery (which Parts~II
and III reuse) and start at Chapter~\ref{ch:us}; a reader who already accepts the
event-based framing can start at Chapter~\ref{ch:poisson} and treat
Chapter~\ref{ch:selection} as the operational summary.

Three reading aids recur throughout. \emph{Warning} environments mark failure modes we
have actually observed --- on real data or on the shipped synthetic testbed --- rather than
theoretical possibilities; skipping them is how offline metrics end up flattering broken
pipelines. \emph{Decision tables} close most chapters and are deliberately opinionated:
they encode not just what a method can do but what it needs from the data to be worth its
complexity. Finally, every quantitative claim carries one of two provenances: a precise
mathematical statement (theorem or proposition, with proof or sketch in
Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}), or a measured number from the synthetic testbed
(consolidated in Chapter~\ref{ch:selection}), regenerable from the \texttt{code/}
directory documented in Appendix~\ref{app:code}.

A note on causal language, since several chapters make causal claims: we keep the
predictive conditional $\E[Y \mid X{=}x]$ and the interventional quantity
$\E[Y \mid \Do(X{=}x)]$ notationally distinct, we state identification assumptions where
they are used, and --- because the testbed's ground truth is known --- we treat the causal
pipeline itself as a testable artifact rather than a rhetorical one. Where identification
is not credible, the text says so and falls back to clearly labelled association.
