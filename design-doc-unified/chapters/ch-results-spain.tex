This chapter is the real-data counterpart of \cref{ch:results-synth}. The target market is the Spanish
venture-capital ecosystem. It is intentionally empty of numbers: each section below receives the
real-data rerun of one protocol of \cref{ch:codeplan}, append-only, under the artifact rules of
\cref{chcp:sec:ci}.

One structural point shapes everything here. Spain is an EU jurisdiction with statutory filing
requirements: companies file annual accounts with the mercantile registry, so firm fundamentals are
observable on a statutory schedule rather than only at deal events. The transparent-regime chapters
(\cref{ch:eufund,ch:eucausal}) therefore apply directly, alongside the deal-based machinery of the
rest of the book. In practice this means registry fundamentals panels can be joined to PitchBook
deals, and the fundamentals-prediction and causal questions can be asked of the same firms the deal
models rank.

\section{Data-assembly checklist}\label{chsp:sec:assembly}

Before any fit, the Spanish extract must pass the following assembly steps. Each is an audit, not a
convenience.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item \emph{Registry fundamentals join.} Join statutory filings to PitchBook company identifiers;
record match rates and the reasons for non-matches. Filing dates, not fiscal-year ends, drive
feature availability.
\item \emph{Deal coverage audit.} Estimate what share of Spanish VC deals the vendor universe misses,
per the model-free audit of \cref{ch11:sec:unseen}; report coverage by era and by sector.
\item \emph{Macro series for the euro area.} The FactSet codes used in \cref{ch:data} are US series;
each has a euro-area analogue (policy rate, effective rate, HICP inflation, unemployment). Joins
remain as-of on publish date.
\item \emph{Point-in-time discipline.} Every joined series --- deals, filings, macro --- passes the
point-in-time protocol of \cref{ch02:sec:pit} before it enters any feature.
\end{itemize}
